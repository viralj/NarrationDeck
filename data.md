**Title:** Log 82-E: The Geometry of Silence
**Word Count:** 1,150 words

**(Sound of heavy static, then the rhythmic thrum of atmosphere scrubbing against the hull. The click of a toggle switch.)**

**LOG STARTS:**

"System check complete. Life support at ninety-eight percent. Thrusters cooling. This is the Void Walker, recording entry for Designation 82-Epsilon."

I’ve been tracking this signal for three weeks across the Outer Rim. It’s not radio. It’s not radiation. It’s... harmonic. A mathematical sequence repeating on a frequency that technically shouldn't exist in a vacuum. Most deep-space signals are screams—dying stars, collapsing pulsars, or the chatter of civilizations looking for a fight. This is different. This feels like a song.

Orbit was turbulent. The ionosphere here is thick, charged with something that makes the nav-computer spit out error codes I’ve never seen before. But looking down... it’s not what the scans promised. The charts said 'Barren Rock.' The window says otherwise. It’s glowing. The whole dark side of the planet is veined with bioluminescence, like a nervous system wrapped around a sphere of obsidian.

I’m bringing the ship down in a valley between two massive geological formations. Or... I think they’re geological. They look less like mountains and more like shards of glass dropped by a giant.

Touchdown. Engine kill. The silence in the cockpit is heavy. Just the cooling fans spinning down. I’m looking at the exterior monitors. The air outside is dense, tinted a deep indigo. It’s twilight, but I don’t think the sun ever truly sets here; the atmosphere is too thick, scattering the starlight into a permanent, bruising purple.

Suiting up. Seals are green. Pressure is stable. I’m grabbing the surveyor kit, but I’m leaving the heavy rifle. Protocol Alpha means we approach with open hands, even if we’re shaking.

I’m out. Gravity is about 0.8 Earth standard. Light. Bounce in the step. The first thing that hits you is the smell—even through the suit’s scrubbers, the sensors are picking up ozone and... cinnamon? It’s spicy. The ground isn’t dirt. It’s a carpet of moss, but every strand is glowing with a faint blue pulse. When I step, the light ripples away from my boot, like splashing in a puddle of photons.

I’m walking toward the source of the signal. It’s about two clicks north, near the base of one of those crystal spires. Up close, these things are massive. Hundreds of meters high. They aren't ice. They’re warm to the thermal scan. They’re vibrating. I can feel it in my teeth. A low hum, a C-sharp resonance that travels through the ground.

The terrain is getting tricky. Sharp angles. The geometry here is aggressive. Rocks aren't rounded by wind; they’re fractured into perfect cubes and pyramids. It’s like the planet is trying to solve an equation.

Wait. Movement. Twelve o’clock.

It’s not an animal. There’s no heat signature. No heartbeat on the audio sensors. It’s just... light.

I’m holding position. I’m crouching behind a ridge of the crystal structures. The target is hovering in a clearing. It’s about seven feet off the ground. It looks like a gyroscope made of solid light—rings within rings, spinning silently. It’s shifting colors. Cyan, magenta, a white so bright it burns the retinas even through the gold visor.

I’m going to step out. Hands visible.

"This is the Void Walker. I mean you no harm."

It reacted. The rings stopped spinning. It’s reorienting. The geometry of the thing is folding in on itself, becoming a dense sphere, then expanding into a fractal star. It’s beautiful and terrifying. It doesn't have eyes, but I feel watched. Scanned. It’s stripping me down to the atomic level with whatever sensor array it has.

I’m stepping closer. The hum is getting louder. My HUD is glitching. Text is scrambling. The suit’s AI is trying to interpret the data stream, but it’s just flooding the buffer with prime numbers.

It’s mimicking me. I raise my hand; a flare of light extends from its center. I take a step back; the light dims. It’s a mirror, but not of form—of intent.

I’m placing the localized beacon on the ground. A peace offering. Just a simple transmitter playing back the Fibonacci sequence. Universal math.

It picked up the beacon. It’s... it’s absorbing it. The light is dissolving the metal. No, not dissolving. Digitizing. The beacon just turned into dust and light, integrated into the entity’s outer ring.

Okay. Okay. It didn't attack. It accepted the data. The colors are shifting to a soft gold, matching my visor. It understands.

This isn't a creature. It’s a custodian. A gardener of physics. These crystal spires... they’re hard drives. This whole valley is a server farm, and this thing is the librarian. It’s showing me images now—projecting them directly onto my visor glass. Star charts. But ancient ones. Constellations that haven't looked like that for a million years. It’s showing me the history of this system.

It’s overwhelming. The density of information is spiking my vitals. My heart rate is one-forty. I can’t—I can’t process this fast.

"Stop. Too much. Too much!"

It stopped. The entity recoiled. The light turned sharp red for a microsecond, then back to neutral white. I think I offended it? Or maybe I just proved I’m too primitive to talk to. It’s drifting away. Rising up toward the apex of the crystal spire.

I’m alone in the clearing again. The moss is still pulsing. The hum is still there, but the melody is gone.

I need to get back to the ship. My suit’s radiation exposure gauge is climbing. Being that close to pure energy has consequences.

Back in the cockpit. Seals engaging.

I’m looking at the data log. We captured about four seconds of that transmission before the overload. It’s more data than humanity has generated in the last decade. It’s going to take the central AI years to decrypt this.

We didn't find a monster. We didn't find a war. We found a library. And I think... I think we just checked out a book we don't know how to read yet.

Thrusters engaging. Let’s get off this rock before the librarian comes back to collect the late fees.

Void Walker out.

**LOG ENDS.**
